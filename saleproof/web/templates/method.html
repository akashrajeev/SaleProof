Method

How SaleProof decides

Every Indian listing shows an M.R.P. and a percentage off it. The M.R.P. is set by the seller, so a big percentage can be made by raising the M.R.P. instead of lowering the price. We ignore the M.R.P. and look at what the product has actually cost.

1. Record prices every day

Once a day we ask SerpApi for Amazon.in search results in four categories and for Google Shopping India results on a few products that other stores also sell. We keep each full response, untouched.

2. Work out the usual price

For each product and day we take the lowest price at a trusted Indian store. The usual price is the median of those daily lows before today. Grey-market importers, spare-part sellers and refurbished listings are recorded but never used.

3. Compare the claim with reality

Fake discountClaims 15% or more off, but the price is less than 5% below the usual price. Or the price was raised shortly before the offer.
Genuine dealAt least 10% below the usual price, whatever the tag says.
Normal priceWithin a few percent of the usual price.
Too early to tellFewer than three days of prices and no other store to compare with.

4. Flag the tricks we can see

  • M.R.P. nobody charges — the M.R.P. is 25% or more above the highest price any trusted store has asked.
  • Different M.R.P. at different stores for the same product.
  • Raised before the sale — the price climbed 8% or more in the days before an offer.
  • Cheaper elsewhere — another trusted store is at least 5% cheaper today.

What this can't see

Bank-card offers, exchange bonuses and coupons applied at checkout are not in the listing price. Prices can change several times a day; we sample once. Different sellers on the same marketplace can price one product differently.
